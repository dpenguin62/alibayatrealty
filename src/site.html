          Overview

          Real estate is more than the purchase price.

          Two properties at the same price can perform very differently once they're rented. What a property earns, what it costs to run, how it's financed and how often it sits empty all decide whether it supports itself or needs money from you every month.

          My approach is the same one I bring to buying and selling: help you make a clearer decision by understanding the numbers behind the property. For an investment, that means putting every assumption on the table, testing the ones that matter most, and being honest about what the numbers can't tell you.

          I'm a licensed real estate professional, not a financial advisor, accountant, lawyer or mortgage broker. The analysis I provide is real estate education and property analysis. It doesn't replace legal, tax, accounting, mortgage or financial advice.

          What I analyze

          Four questions behind every property.

          01 · Income

          What can it earn?

          Current rental income and potential rental income, checked against comparable rentals nearby rather than the listing's projections.

          02 · Expenses

          What does it cost to run?

          Property taxes, insurance, utilities, maintenance, vacancy and other operating costs, including the ones a seller's summary tends to leave out.

          03 · Financing

          How will it be paid for?

          Down payment, mortgage assumptions, interest rate and debt service: the monthly cost of borrowing and how sensitive the result is to it.

          04 · Returns

          What's left over?

          Net operating income (NOI), cap rate, cash flow and cash-on-cash return, each explained in plain language so you know what it measures and what it doesn't.

          Property types

          Income properties come in many shapes.

          Each type has its own rents, costs, rules and buyer pool when it's time to sell. These are the kinds of properties I can help you evaluate.

          Property use, zoning, financing and rental arrangements should always be independently verified before a purchase decision. A property being marketed as a duplex, a legal suite or an income property doesn't by itself confirm its legal status.

            Buying guide

            Land transfer tax in Toronto vs. York Region

            Why the same purchase price costs more to close on in North York than in Vaughan, Richmond Hill or Thornhill, and how to estimate yours.

            AB
            Ali Bayat
            Rates verified against official sources on October 1, 2026 · Reviewed by Ali

            Every buyer in Ontario pays the provincial land transfer tax when a purchase closes. If the home is in the City of Toronto, which includes North York, Scarborough and Etobicoke, you also pay Toronto's municipal land transfer tax. Buyers in York Region communities such as Vaughan, Richmond Hill and Thornhill pay only the provincial tax.

            On a $1,000,000 home, that difference is about $16,475. It's one of the most important numbers to know when you're comparing homes on either side of Steeles Avenue.

            Estimate your land transfer tax

            $
            Where is the home?
            Ontario land transfer tax—
            Ontario first-time buyer rebate—
            Toronto municipal land transfer tax—
            Estimated total—

            For a home with one or two single-family residences, using the Ontario rates and the City of Toronto rates in effect from April 1, 2026. This is an estimate, not legal or tax advice; your real estate lawyer confirms the final amount.

            How the two taxes are calculated

            Both taxes are charged in brackets, so each portion of the price is taxed at its own rate. Up to $2,000,000 the Ontario and Toronto rates are the same, which is why buying in Toronto roughly doubles the tax. Above $3,000,000, Toronto's rates rise more steeply.

            First-time buyer rebates

            Ontario refunds up to $4,000 of the provincial tax for eligible first-time buyers. The City of Toronto has its own first-time buyer rebate of up to $4,475 on the municipal tax. Eligibility rules apply to both, so confirm yours with your lawyer before you budget for it.

            What this means when you're choosing where to buy

            If you're deciding between, say, a condo near Finch station and one near the Vaughan Metropolitan Centre, the land transfer tax is part of the real price. I include it in every side-by-side comparison I prepare for clients, together with legal fees, adjustments and moving costs.
